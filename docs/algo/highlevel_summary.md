# Native-resolution vector preparation

**Prepare** shows the reference, the completed vector artwork, and the polygon sequence. The sequence slider reveals whole pours in paint order. Scroll over either vector view to inspect an edge, drag to pan, and double-click to fit the canvas. **Plot** previews the separate 4 mm brush plan. A polygon can be visible in Prepare even when it is narrower than a brush stroke.

## Color and geometry

1. Count exact source RGB values across the entire decoded image. The palette limit is 2–1,024, with 64 as the default. An image already within the limit keeps its exact colors. For a larger histogram, partition colors in Oklab space by weighted variance. Each split searches the highest-variance axis for the greatest reduction in squared error. Square-root frequency weighting gives rare accents more influence. Two nearest-palette refinements reduce partition artifacts, and a kd-tree accelerates assignment. Every original pixel receives a label.
2. Find four-connected components of each label. Trace oriented boundaries with filled material on the right. At diagonal contacts the walker turns right, keeping diagonally touching islands distinct. Trace inner boundaries as well as outer ones. Each patch stores its palette entry and closed rings in a shared vertex pool. The signed sum of its ring areas gives its net painted area.
3. At an ordinary two-color turn, join the two edge midpoints. The neighboring patch uses the same endpoints. Image boundaries, three-color junctions, and diagonal contacts keep their original corners. Straight runs collapse to their endpoints. This gives diagonals continuous vector edges while preserving ownership of every source pixel center. A one-pixel isolated highlight can become a diamond. Its own polygon survives.
4. Paint patches in descending area bands. Each band spans a factor of two in area. Within a band, group shades into 12 Oklab hue sectors and a separate low-chroma group, then paint each group from low to high lightness. Exact area and discovery order break remaining ties. This gives broad base regions priority and allows a shadow to precede a slightly larger highlight. When a hole contains exactly one later, hole-free island, the surrounding pour paints through it. The island then overpaints that base. Composite holes retain their boundaries.

## Display and toolpaths

The renderer builds a scanline index directly from the rings on the preparation worker. Edge endpoints lie on the half-pixel lattice. Between consecutive lattice rows, edge order stays fixed and each fill boundary varies linearly. The vector control clips these intervals to its viewport and caches four polygon IDs per display pixel. Scrubbing recolors that cache. Zooming or resizing samples the geometry again. The artwork scales independently of the brush width and the original image's display size. Fine detail still depends on the information present in the reference and the selected palette.

The physical planner samples the vector fills at the centers of 4 mm brush cells and joins adjacent cells belonging to the same polygon into serpentine runs. Strokes follow polygon order and keep the nominal brush footprint inside the canvas. The planner includes undercoat strokes beneath eligible islands, and Plot shows their color before the overpaint. A brush footprint can cover a narrow edge or miss a small island. The Plot simulation makes that approximation visible. CMYKW fractions remain uncalibrated mixing estimates.

The CLI command `vectors` writes the visible polygon prefix as SVG with compound even-odd paths, an aspect-preserving viewBox, and the selected physical canvas dimensions. Prepare initially selects all polygons. Use `seek-patch N` for a partial SVG. The existing `seek N`, `plan`, and `preview` commands address the physical stroke sequence.

## Research behind the choices

- Wu describes variance-driven palette partitioning. PainterBot applies a histogram split in Oklab with a different weighting and refinement policy. Oklab provides a lightness coordinate and two opponent-color coordinates for color matching and shade ordering. [Wu, Graphics Gems II](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-08-050754-5.50035-9) · [Ottosson, Oklab](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)
- Selinger describes boundary decomposition, ambiguous turns, and polygon approximation in Potrace. PainterBot uses an explicit turn rule and retains single-pixel components. GDAL's polygonization model also groups connected pixels sharing a label and documents the potential for dense geometry in photographs. [Potrace paper](https://www.mathstat.dal.ca/~selinger/potrace/potrace.pdf) · [GDAL polygonization](https://gdal.org/en/stable/api/gdal_alg.html)
- VTracer describes clustering, path walking, simplification, and curve fitting. For physical pours we chose shared straight edges and explicit holes so the same geometry defines neighboring fills, the displayed artwork, and SVG export. [VTracer design](https://www.visioncortex.org/vtracer-docs/)
